# Backlog (Phase 1 synthesis, draft 2026-10-27, research items 2026-10-28)

Ranked by measured cost, then feasibility and risk. Measurements are Wine on an M4 (calibration quality; see `docs/harness/bench.md`), XL scenario unless stated. "Measured" means a benchmark or census number exists. "Code" means the behaviour is confirmed in source but its cost is not yet measured. Correctness repairs and behaviour-preserving optimizations follow different acceptance rules (unpublished notes, review round 2, item 2).

## Next by measured share (scenario xl-v4, 2026-10-29)

The v4 reference profiles (`docs/atlas/profiles-2026-10-28.md`, section "Scenario xl-v4 reference profiles") rank what is left, both variants alike: item filter volume about 15% of Lua samples (RS1), the restock director 12 to 14% (a rating cache with exact invalidation), inventory and trackers about 5%, timers and calendar about 4% self and 10% inclusive, task actions 2 to 3.5% self and about 15% inclusive (RS8), swimming about 2% inclusive (a check skipped for entities far from any water), and save time 3.5 to 4.6 s per late-game save (S10). Water itself adds 0.5% (hydrology with four still lakes). The maintainer picks the next item from this list.

Done since: the material check memo (experiment 009, Steady Hearth 1.2.0) cut material checks from 4.1% to 2.8% (vanilla) and 7.3% to 5.1% (ACE). The restock director's two dead caches (an item rating cache that is filled but never read, and an errand validity cache that expires after 20 ms because of a unit mix-up) are confirmed in code, but turning them on changed which errands hearthlings took and the runs with them did less town work, so both ship off by default. New lever found on the way: the game reads hot tables with `rawget`, which under the shipped PUC Lua 5.1 is a C call about three times the cost of a plain index. Next candidates: a survey of hot `rawget` sites by measured share (behaviour-preserving where the table has no metatable), and single-change A/B pairs for the two restock caches.

Known ACE workload difference (the scenario works as intended): under ACE the scenario's maintain orders leave the crafter lists before the measured window, and the crafters work on fixture orders and ingredient chains that mostly lack ingredients, so ACE crafts 4 or 5 items per town per game day against 14 or 15 in vanilla (`docs/harness/bench.md`, scenario xl-v4).

First RS1 candidate, B1 (checked in code 2026-10-28): `fabricate_chunk` searches for `stonehearth:build2:entities:chunk` entities owned by the work player (`fabricate_chunk_action.lua:24-31`). With no construction, which is most of a real game, every worker's finder exhausts the world and restarts every 2.5 s of real time. Chunks are created in three places (`new_chunk_node.lua:89`, `reduce_scaffolding_node.lua:71`, `scaffolding_set.lua:102`), all through `radiant.entities.create_entity` with an owner, and the engine fires `radiant:entity:post_create` and `radiant:entity:post_destroy` globally. A per-player set of live chunk ids kept from those events would let `_start_item_finder` wait for a chunk instead of searching. Open: whether `post_create` fires for entities restored from a save after the patch installs (the food decay service relies on it during load), and a benchmark phase without construction (the XL scenario keeps construction going in every town), for example `live_buildings=0`.

Not adopted: native pathfinding algorithm changes (JPS, HPA*, D* Lite, flow fields) are out of reach from Lua; parallel simulation is gated on the measured job-list share (Factorio FFF-215 found a parallel update slower than the serial one). `CatalogService:is_material` is already memoized per material string, so its remaining cost is the number of filter callbacks, which RS1 addresses.
